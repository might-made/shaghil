A. Primary Arabic Wordmark

شغّل
شغّل
Small size (~24px row height): شغّل

Set in Tajawal, a clean, slightly condensed modern Arabic face — the ش's three dots sit tight and compact, the غ's descender is short and controlled, and the shadda is a small precise mark rather than an ornamental one. The whole word reads as one fast, confident horizontal shape rather than a sequence of separately-noticed letters — built for a tight, quick read, not for lingering.

B. English Wordmark

SHAGHIL

Sora, mixed case, slightly tightened tracking — crisp and geometric without Direction 01's engineered heaviness. Set smaller than the Arabic wordmark in every paired lockup.

C. Symbol / Mark

A single bold diagonal stroke — a fast, decisive "go" mark — with a small horizontal notch beneath it hinting at ش's base stroke. The diagonal alone is the carrying shape; the notch is a secondary detail.

Why this pairing: Tajawal is already a well-regarded choice across contemporary Gulf digital products for exactly this "fast, native, unornamented" register, and its slightly condensed width helps dense Arabic UI copy (button labels, status lines) stay compact. Inter — the product's current Latin body face — is retained entirely unchanged, the lowest-disruption typographic choice of the three directions; Sora is added only for display-weight headlines where Inter alone reads too neutral. (Note: the brand strategy document named "Neue Montreal" as an alternative display option; Sora is used here for reliable free-CDN delivery — see the accompanying markdown for this disclosure.)

J. "by MIGHT MADE" Lockup

شغّل by MIGHT MADE

"by MIGHT MADE" set as plain text only — small, muted, letter-spaced — never as a reconstructed MIGHT MADE mark. This direction's near-monochrome base is the closest of the three to MIGHT MADE's own disciplined charcoal/off-white language, which is worth flagging explicitly as both an opportunity (credible shared DNA) and a risk (needs to stay clearly distinct, not merged).

L. Motion Principle — Directional / Fast

Elements move a short, fixed distance along the mark's diagonal, fast and with no overshoot — a "go" cue, not a decorative flourish. Loading is a short directional sweep rather than a circular spinner; transitions are the shortest of the three directions.
